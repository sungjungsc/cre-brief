01

Capital markets & macro

The 10-year Treasury held above the psychological 5.00% level all week, touching a 12-month high of 5.18% on September 24 before easing 1bp to 5.17% by the 25th — confirming last week's break above 5% is a durable shift, not a one-day spike. The Fed's September 16 hike to 3.75%–4.00% remains the last policy move (no meeting this week), with markets still pricing one more 25bp hike before year-end. Post-Jackson-Hole commentary (Fed Chair Warsh) continues to work through CRE underwriting: lenders are now advised to model off a 10-year UST in the low-to-mid 5% range rather than the mid-4.70s assumption that prevailed in early July, and Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed CRE mortgage rate remains at 6.76% — the highest CRE borrowing cost in over a year. This keeps the earlier-2026 CBRE thesis of 5–15bps of 2026 cap-rate compression in question. Sources: 09-16, 09-24/25

CRE debt remains in active repricing mode (Freddie Mac 30-yr CRE 6.76%); refinancings and new originations in this rate range warrant close underwriting review.
