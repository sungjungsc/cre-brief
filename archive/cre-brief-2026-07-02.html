01

Capital markets & macro

The 10-year Treasury eased to 4.47% after testing 4.50% intraday, as new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh flagged softening inflation risk. The Fed held its benchmark at 3.50–3.75% at the June 17 meeting under a hawkish tone — the dot plot now splits, with 9 of 19 officials projecting at least one more 2026 hike — and futures price a 79.5% chance of another hold at the July 28–29 meeting, pending the June CPI print due July 14. CRE debt markets stayed broadly steady: average cap rates (~6.34%) sit just under average borrowing costs (~6.57%), a historically tight spread, with liquidity ample but underwriting discipline unchanged against roughly $1 trillion of 2026 loan maturities. Sources: 07-02, 06-17, CBRE/Northmarq 2026 outlooks

05

Watch items

  1. Sunbelt oversupply is still working through the system — Austin, San Antonio, Dallas and Charlotte all show elevated vacancy or record concessions, though San Antonio and Austin deliveries are falling sharply in 2026, setting up for a 2027 recovery.
  2. The Fed's hawkish dot plot (9 of 19 officials eyeing a 2026 hike) keeps refinancing risk elevated ahead of ~$1 trillion in 2026 loan maturities — watch the July 28–29 FOMC and the June CPI print due July 14.
  3. Charlotte concessions are at a record high (>50% of properties) with recent deliveries leasing at just 72% after 15 months — absorption pace bears watching before calling a bottom.
  4. Student pre-lease pace (RealPage 68.2%, Yardi 71.6% as of April) is running ahead of decade averages, but campus-level signal was thin this week for GSU, UTSA, UCSB, UF and USC-Columbia — a data gap worth closing next cycle.
  5. Hotel city-level detail (LA/Burbank, Austin, OKC) was not obtainable this week due to source access issues; only stale national weekly STR figures were available.
