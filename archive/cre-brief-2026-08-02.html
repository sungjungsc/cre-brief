Capital markets & macro

The 10-year Treasury pulled back sharply to roughly 4.22–4.24% by Aug 1–2, a ~45 bp drop from the 4.67–4.69% high touched two weeks ago, even as the Jul 29 FOMC produced an unusually hawkish split: the Fed held at 3.50–3.75% for a 5th consecutive meeting, but three governors (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) dissented in favor of a rate hike — the first time since September 2016 that three policymakers have united behind a hawkish dissent. Multifamily cap rates held at 5.7–5.8% blended, a plateau now in its 7th consecutive quarter — the longest streak in 25 years — with wide market dispersion (San Francisco 3.88% vs. Fort Lauderdale 6.27%) and gradual 2026 compression still the consensus view. Watch items

  1. The 10-year Treasury reversed sharply (−45 bps to ~4.23%) even as the Jul 29 FOMC delivered an unusually hawkish 9–3 split — three dissents favoring a hike, the first unified hawkish 3-dissent since 2016 — a divergence between the bond market's read and the Fed's internal debate worth tracking into the next decision.
  2. The rent-growth leaderboard's underlying index changed this week (Apartment List's own ranks didn't surface; CoStar/Apartments.com filled the gap instead). San Antonio (−3.0%) and Austin (−1.9%, tied with Las Vegas) remain the two weakest oversupply metros, but the rank shift vs. last week's Apartment List-basis reads (−5.0%, −4.3%) reflects a methodology change, not a market move.
  3. Charlotte's concession rate climbed again (60.6% in Q2, up from 58.0% in Q1) even as absorption hit a quarterly peak (~4,800 net move-ins) — a market still working through the 2nd-largest active supply pipeline in the country.
  4. Hotel sector outlook is diverging: CoStar/Tourism Economics just upgraded the FY2026 national RevPAR forecast to +2.8% (from +0.6%), yet LA remains the only major market with negative YoY occupancy, tied to the phased $30/hr hotel-worker wage increase.
  5. Student housing's national RealPage pace jumped to 84.7% pre-leased as of June (filling a 2-month data gap), while 7 of 8 tracked campuses went a second consecutive week without a fresh search — worth refreshing next cycle before staleness compounds further.
