Capital markets & macro

The 10-year Treasury spiked sharply this week, touching a 19-month high of 4.75% intraweek before settling near 4.65% by Friday — a jump of roughly 40+ bps from last week's steady 4.22–4.24% range, as inflation-complacency concerns resurfaced alongside rising energy prices. There was no FOMC meeting this week (rates remain held at 3.50–3.75%; next meeting September 16); one report referenced a "Fed Chairman Warsh" comment on the yield move, unconfirmed by a second source and flagged for follow-up rather than treated as settled. CBRE's newly published H1 2026 Cap Rate Survey found cap rates broadly flat across sectors despite the Treasury move, with Class B/C and value-add assets compressing faster than Class A/stabilized. Watch items

  1. The 10-year Treasury's spike to a 19-month high (4.75% intraweek) is the sharpest macro move in weeks — worth watching whether cap rates begin to reflect it despite CBRE's "flat" H1 read.
  2. Hotel sector outlook turned notably more bullish this week (FY2026 RevPAR forecast raised to +4.4% from +2.8%, boosted by World Cup premiums) — a positive contrast to Austin's persistent softness.
  3. USC-Columbia's Gateway 737 going fully leased (waitlist only) for Fall '26–'27 is a fresh tightening-supply signal worth tracking alongside the Charlotte/Dallas/Austin/San Antonio oversupply picture.
  4. Sunbelt supply cycle continues to unwind — Austin and San Antonio 2026 delivery guidance were both revised down again this week, setting up easing rent pressure into 2027.
  5. A single-source reference to "Fed Chairman Warsh" this week is unconfirmed — needs a second source before treating any Fed leadership change as fact.
