Capital markets & macro

The 10-year Treasury eased to 4.67% by Friday (−7 bps on the week) after touching 4.74% the prior week, even as rate-move expectations flipped sharply: futures now price a 25-basis-point HIKE at the September 15–16 FOMC meeting — a reversal from recent cut hopes — as Iran-related energy-supply shocks push inflation higher and test the Fed's credibility. The Fed itself held at 3.50–3.75% at its July 28–29 meeting (9-3 vote, three dissents favoring a hike). CBRE's H1 2026 Cap Rate Survey, published this month, found cap rates broadly flat despite the Treasury volatility (10-yr peaked 4.67% mid-May, ~4.6% mid-July), though transaction volumes remain subdued — 77% of CBRE Capital Markets professionals said the US/Iran conflict has hurt their 2026 investment-activity outlook, describing a "pricing stalemate." Watch items

  1. Rate-move sentiment reversed this week — markets now price a 25bp Fed HIKE at the September 15–16 FOMC (Iran-driven energy inflation cited) rather than the cuts recently expected; the 10-yr eased slightly to 4.67%, but the September meeting is now a real financing-cost risk event for the portfolio.
  2. Charlotte (−3.2%) and Austin (−5.9%) remain the steepest YoY decliners among watched metros even as both show tentative stabilization (Charlotte's pipeline thinning, Austin's Q1 demand exceeding supply) — too early to call a bottom in either.
  3. San Antonio's 2026 delivery forecast (just 2,600 units, −63% vs. 2025, slowest since 2011) and Charlotte's sharply reduced construction starts point to a 2026–27 supply-driven recovery window across the Sunbelt/Southeast held metros.
  4. Fall 2026 student pre-lease is running ahead of decadal averages nationally (RealPage 84.7% June, Yardi 89.1% July) even as UT Austin, UTSA, and USC-Columbia all see meaningful new competitive supply delivering this fall — watch fall-semester absorption at those three campuses specifically.
  5. Austin hotel softness (RevPAR −6.3%, occupancy −15%) is a convention-center-closure story running through 2029, not a broad demand issue — keep it distinct from the metro's apartment-market oversupply narrative when reading Austin headlines.
