Capital markets & macro

The 10-year Treasury touched a two-month high of 4.62% on Jul 13 before easing back to 4.55% by Jul 17, as softer inflation data reduced near-term rate-hike odds — net, the week landed roughly flat versus last week's 4.54–4.56% range. Multifamily cap rates were unchanged at the Q1 plateau (5.6% avg across classes), now seven consecutive quarters near this level — the longest such streak in 25 years — with gradual compression still expected through 2026. Watch items

  1. Oversupply cluster is diverging: Austin and San Antonio deliveries are collapsing with demand now outpacing new supply, but Charlotte is moving the other way — Northmarq now calls it the most supply-exposed major metro nationally at year-end 2025, with 21,780 units still under construction.
  2. Rent-estimate divergence across aggregators remains wide this week: Dallas spans −9% to +2.8% YoY depending on source, and Columbus spans −0.8% to +3.3% — treat single-source metro rent figures with caution.
  3. The 10-year Treasury spiked to a two-month high (4.62%) mid-week before easing to 4.55%, even as the Fed heads into its Jul 29 decision still internally split — financing costs remain more volatile than the "hold" headline suggests.
  4. Student housing margin risk persists: Yardi's 2026–27 leasing-season rent growth of just +0.9% YoY (vs. +2.6% prior cycle) even as pre-lease pace holds near decade averages — this reads as a rate-growth risk, not an occupancy risk.
  5. Two data-continuity gaps worth flagging rather than treating as reversals: Michigan's previously-cited 115,000-application enrollment surge and USC-Columbia's VERVE Columbia/Gateway 737 supply items did not resurface in this week's searches.
