01

Capital markets & macro

The 10-year Treasury held near 5.28% at the 10-02 close (intraday range 5.16–5.34%; 5.34% was the highest since 2002) after a weak September jobs report (+29k payrolls, unemployment 4.2%) cut odds of an October Fed hike to roughly 14–17%. The Fed funds range is 3.75–4.00% following the 09-16 hike; September minutes show most participants still expect another increase by year-end, with the next FOMC on Oct 27–28. First American puts the all-asset transaction cap rate near 6.3% (Q2) with the spread over the 10-year around 1.6 pts at mid-August yields, near the 6th percentile since 1991. Since then yields have risen, so the spread is likely narrower. CBRE's H1 survey found cap rates roughly unchanged, with more respondents now expecting increases. 30-yr mortgage rates reached ~7.45%. Sources: TradingEconomics 10-02, Scotsman Guide/FXStreet 10-02–10-08, First American, CBRE (Q2/H1, as of 08-15)

Watch items

  1. Rates: 10-yr near 5.3% with the cap-rate spread at historic lows; Oct 27–28 FOMC (hold priced ~83%) and the Nov 3 midterms are the next catalysts. Watch for cap-rate repricing.
  2. Sunbelt supply: deliveries are falling (US H1 −41%), but concessions remain heavy (Austin 70.7% of listings; San Antonio 31–68% depending on source) and vacancy sits near 9% in Austin, Charlotte and Dallas.
  3. Apartment List national rent −0.1% MoM in September (YoY −0.4%, up from a −1.6% low in April); vacancy 7.0%. Check whether the off-season dip deepens in the October release.
  4. Student housing: pre-leasing near record, but Yardi shows rent growth slowing to +1.1% and early enrollment data softer, partly on international. Fall final-occupancy data due.
  5. Hotels: recent weekly RevPAR gains (+13–14%) are inflated by holiday shifts and events; read the Oct comps with that in mind.
