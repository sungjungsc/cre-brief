Capital markets & macro

The 10-year Treasury jumped to roughly 4.67–4.69% by Jul 24 (snippet reads diverge slightly on the exact print), up meaningfully from the 4.55% low touched Jul 17 — a ~13 bp weekly rise as oil prices surged above $100/barrel and pushed the odds of a later-2026 Fed hike to 38%, up sharply from just 12% a week earlier. The Fed remains on hold at 3.50–3.75% heading into its Jul 28–29 FOMC decision (no Summary of Economic Projections at this meeting), with a hold still the base case despite the shifting inflation risk. Multifamily cap rates held at their Q1 plateau (5.6–5.8% blended across classes), with wide market dispersion (San Francisco 3.88% vs. Fort Lauderdale 6.27%) and consensus still expecting gradual compression through 2026 as credit conditions ease. Watch items

  1. Financing-cost risk stepped up this week: the 10-year jumped ~13 bps to a fresh high near 4.68% and later-2026 Fed hike odds tripled to 38% (from 12%) on an oil-price surge, right ahead of the Jul 29 FOMC decision.
  2. Houston and Indianapolis both got their first Apartment List-anchored reads in 5 weeks — and both came in negative (−2.8%, −0.7%), breaking from the "modest growth" story these two metros carried in prior weeks.
  3. Charlotte's supply picture is intensifying rather than easing: 18,000 units under construction (2nd-largest active pipeline in the US) and roughly 65% of listings now offering concessions.
  4. National hotel data quality diverged sharply this week — three fresh weekly STR releases resolved the prior stale flag nationally, while Oklahoma City and LA/Burbank remain dark for a 5th consecutive week with no city-level actuals.
  5. Two reads flagged as unresolved discrepancies rather than trend reversals: Atlanta's rent figure swung from a reported +4.69% (Zumper) last week to roughly flat this week, and San Antonio's vacancy reading dropped sharply (15.8% → 9.1%) without a clear methodology bridge between sources.
