Capital markets & macro

The 10-year Treasury extended its climb to 4.74% by Friday (+9 bps on the week), with commentary pointing to soaring AI-related debt issuance and higher federal deficit spending, alongside Treasury debt-buyback developments, as the drivers. There was no FOMC meeting in August — the fed funds rate remains held at 3.50–3.75% since December 2025, with the July 28–29 decision passing 9-3 (three regional presidents dissenting in favor of a hike); minutes were released 08-19, the Fed's preferred PCE inflation gauge is due 08-26, and Fed Chair Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole speech 08-28 — all inputs to the next vote on 09-16. CBRE's H1 2026 Cap Rate Survey found cap rates broadly flat across sectors even as the 10-yr peaked at 4.67% intra-period, though stabilized NYC multifamily cap rates widened roughly 50 bps to 5.0–5.5%; CBRE's full-year outlook still calls for 5–15 bps of compression, concentrated in higher-quality assets. Watch items

  1. The 10-year Treasury extended its climb to 4.74% while CRE cap rates stayed broadly flat per CBRE's H1 2026 survey — a widening gap between financing cost and asset pricing worth tracking into September's FOMC.
  2. Charlotte (−3.2%), San Antonio (−2.9%), Houston (−2.3% effective), and Dallas (−1.6%) all posted negative YoY multifamily rent growth this week — Sunbelt/Southeast oversupply digestion remains the dominant multifamily theme, with Charlotte not expected to turn positive until 2027.
  3. Austin's Q2 rent uptick (+1.3% QoQ, first gain since fall 2022) is a tentative easing signal, but Class B/C rents remain sharply negative (−3.9%/−11.6% YoY) — too early to call the bottom.
  4. Fall 2026 student pre-lease (RealPage 84.7% as of June) is running behind the fastest prior years at several campuses seeing heavy new competitive supply (UT Austin, UTSA, USC-Columbia) — watch fall-semester absorption.
