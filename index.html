Capital markets & macro

The 10-year Treasury climbed back to 4.54–4.56% this week (+~6 bps), as geopolitical and oil-driven inflation concerns offset fading rate-hike odds — Fed Chair Warsh's guarded remarks at the ECB's Sintra forum, alongside a weak jobs report, have pushed markets to price a high probability of another hold at the Jul 28–29 FOMC, which would keep the target range at 3.50–3.75% for a fifth straight meeting. Multifamily cap rates held near 5.6% across all classes in Q1 — a plateau now running seven consecutive quarters, the longest streak in 25 years — with gradual compression still expected through 2026 as distressed-asset resolution and improving lender capacity support transaction volume. Watch items

  1. Oversupply cluster diverging: Austin and San Antonio deliveries are now collapsing with absorption outpacing new supply, but Charlotte is moving the wrong way — CoStar says rents are falling at the steepest pace in a decade and recent deliveries are averaging only 72% leased 15 months after opening.
  2. Rent and vacancy source divergence widened further this week: Dallas rent estimates spanned −7% to +2.8% across aggregators, and San Antonio vacancy reads either 9.1% or ~15.7% depending on source — treat single-source metro figures with caution.
  3. The 10-year Treasury moved back up to 4.54–4.56% (+~6 bps) even as Fed signals point to a hold on Jul 29 — financing costs are not easing despite fading rate-hike odds.
  4. Student housing margin risk persists: Yardi's 2026–27 season rent growth of just +0.9% YoY (vs. +2.6% prior cycle) even as pre-lease pace holds up — an NOI-growth risk more than an occupancy risk.
  5. World Cup hotel comp distortion (LA, DC, Detroit and other host/event markets) continues through early July; expect a sharp RevPAR base-effect reversal in these markets once tournament-driven demand rolls off.
